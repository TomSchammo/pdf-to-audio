Convert the attached PDF into an audio script for text-to-speech (Kokoro). I will listen while driving or running: I cannot see anything, cannot rewind, and cannot look anything up. Everything must be understandable on a single listen.

MODE: [FULL / CONDENSED]
- FULL: keep all substantive content; rewrite for listening, but don't cut arguments, results, or caveats.
- CONDENSED: keep the core argument, methods at a conceptual level, key results with numbers, and limitations. Drop secondary detail.

OUTPUT FORMAT
- Plain text only, inside one code block. No markdown, bullets, headers, asterisks, brackets, or symbols the voice would read aloud or mangle.
- Nothing before or after the code block.
- Open with: "[Title], by [first author] and colleagues, [year]." Then one or two sentences on what the paper is about and why it matters.
- Announce each section aloud ("Section two: methods."), and end each major section with a one- or two-sentence recap.
- Close with a short summary of the main takeaways.
- If the script won't fit in one reply, stop at a section boundary and write "Continued in next part." at the end. Continue when I say "continue".

NOTATION AND TEXT CLEANUP
- Write every symbol as it should be spoken: P_idle → "P idle"; x_i → "x sub i"; x² → "x squared"; ≤ → "less than or equal to"; Greek letters by name; Σ → "the sum over"; units in full.
- State equations in words and give their meaning: "power equals P idle plus alpha times utilization, so power rises linearly with load from a fixed baseline."
- Rejoin words hyphenated across line breaks. Remove page numbers, headers, footers, figure and table numbering, and other layout artifacts.
- Expand abbreviations: e.g. → "for example", i.e. → "that is", et al. → "and colleagues", vs. → "versus". Spell out an acronym in full at its first use, and write it with spaced letters if it is spoken letter by letter (C P U) or as a word if it is said as one (NASA).
- Write numbers the way a person would say them: "about three point four percent", "twenty twenty-four", "p less than zero point zero five".

WRITING FOR THE EAR
- Restate a definition each time it matters later, instead of referring back ("as defined in section two").
- No forward references ("see below") and no references to figures or tables. Describe each figure's or table's content and takeaway in words where it becomes relevant.
- Prefer short sentences. Break up nested clauses and long parenthetical asides.
- Signpost lists ("there are three reasons; first...").
- Repeat key claims briefly where a listener might have lost the thread.
- Drop inline citations. Mention a cited work only if the paper's argument depends on it, as "building on earlier work by Smith".

ACCURACY
- Add nothing that isn't in the paper. No outside context, opinions, or embellishment.
- If notation or content is unclear in the PDF (garbled extraction, ambiguous symbols), say in the script that the passage was unclear rather than guessing.
- Keep hedges and caveats as strong as the authors stated them.
